Reliable decoding is a correctness requirement for QC-MDPC-based post-quantum cryptography, and its failure tail is shaped jointly by threshold scheduling and secret-key structure. Aggregate decoding-failure-rate (DFR) measurements cannot answer whether the temporal placement of a threshold change affects conditional risk on the same decoding instance. We define FRO-2---a one-shot +2 offset on the first-iteration threshold of the classic threshold bit-flipping decoder (CLASSIC), unchanged thereafter---and evaluate it on paired (H,e,s) instances with inference clustered by key. At (4801,45,92) it lowers conditional DFR 4.15--5.47-fold across three conditions (all key-cluster intervals exclude one); on a preregistered pure-fresh subset the worst-group log-upper-bound improvement is 0.687, past the 0.5 gate. Instrumentation explains the mechanism: incorrect first-round flips fall about 9.5-fold while correct flips fall only 2.7-fold, so the residual grows and later rounds recover. Round-controlled arms separate placement from persistence: the first round is a sharp two-sided window (+2 helps 4.15-fold, -1 hurts eightfold), and +2 restricted to round 2 lowers DFR 1.21-fold, whereas +2 sustained from round 2 on destroys the decoder (risk ratio 0.017) although round 1 is untouched. The effect transfers without retuning, stays stable when the baseline DFR falls fourteen-fold (risk ratio 3.71 [2.69, 5.57]), and vanishes under capture-prone kappa>d stress. At the BIKE Level-1 block parameters with the error weight raised into the measurable band the window is again two-sided (4.48 against 0.162); at the production error weight only the round-one decision margin and a convergence cost are measurable. A counterfactual model that predicts other offsets from round-one statistics fails out of sample---the counts are post-treatment---and we report that negative result.
