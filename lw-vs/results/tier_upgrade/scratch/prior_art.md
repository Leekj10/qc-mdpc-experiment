# Prior-art scan (FINAL v2, 2026-10-07): counterfactual round-1 surrogate for QC-MDPC BF decoder (FRO-2)

Question under test: is it novel to fit, at ONE operating point, a per-instance model P(fail | round-1 features: #true flips, #false flips, post-round-1 syndrome weight) — features computable without running the counterfactual arm — and use it to predict the risk ratio of other first-round threshold offsets (FRO-2 = +2 first-iteration only) and other operating points (incl. BIKE L1 r=12323, d=71, t=134 where DFR is unmeasurable)?

Access notes: bikesuite.org has an EXPIRED TLS cert (curl schannel: SEC_E_CERT_EXPIRED); eprint.iacr.org is Cloudflare-gated for curl; HAL serves an Anubis bot-wall; web.archive.org unreachable from this host. Consequently the BIKE v5.2 spec PDF itself could not be opened directly; its content below is reconstructed from the Sendrier NIST seminar, the layered-BF paper, the CiC paper, the Barekeng v5.2 analysis, the Luebeck thesis, and search-engine quotes of the spec PDF. ## Q1. Predictive models of decoder failure from early-round state

Direct per-instance failure prediction EXISTS (soft-decision LDPC):
- **T. Kim, J. Park, "Logistic Regression for LDPC Decoding Failure Prediction", IEEE CCNC 2021**, Las Vegas, Jan 9-12 2021, pp. 1-6. DOI 10.1109/CCNC49032.2021.9369532. Per-instance logistic-regression classifier; features = (iteration index, #unsatisfied parity checks); threshold-based early-stop rule; can stop at iteration 1; uses a quarter of parity checks for complexity; NR LDPC BG2, K=520, R=1/3, sum-product. PURPOSE: early termination, not counterfactual risk ratios; not BF/MDPC; single configuration (no cross-config transfer claimed).
- Related: DNN on normalized LLR histogram for early termination, IEEE WCNC 2021, DOI 10.1109/WCNC49053.2021.9417420. Patents US20180343082A1 / US20240283467A1: syndrome-weight threshold + rate-of-change early abort.
- **arXiv 2609.26215, "When to Stop? Dynamic Early Termination of Sequential Ensembles" (2026)**: risk-controlled termination; decision tree estimates residual risk; per-member thresholds fitted OFFLINE to a prescribed FER loss; BP+OSD ensembles on 802.11 LDPC; 39x BP work reduction. Parallel for "offline-fitted risk model used as a runtime decision rule" (not per-instance failure-odds across configurations).

MDPC-specific early-round use: surrogate STATISTICS rather than per-instance probabilities:
- **T. B. Paiva, R. Terada, "PickyFix" TCHES 2022** (IACR TCHES, download 9815/9320; CHES 2022 version: ime.usp.br PDF). Section 4.1 "BGF's First Iteration": uses "the average number of uncorrected errors left after the first iteration" (curves BGF1(r), PickyFix1(r)) as a cheap surrogate for DFR when comparing decoders across r: "instead of counting decoding failures, we count the average number of uncorrected errors left, which can be estimated with a much smaller sample". This is the closest published "cheap first-round surrogate statistic" — but population-level, not per-instance odds, and no counterfactual risk ratio.
- **N. Sendrier, V. Vasseur, "On the Decoding Failure Rate of QC-MDPC Bit-Flipping Decoders", PQCrypto 2019** (LNCS; hal-03139797): Markov chain over (S = syndrome weight, t = error weight) + failure state; DFR dominated by the first round failing to reduce error count; syndrome bits independent in first round; explicitly shows error-weight-only models fail for MDPC (uniform-error LDPC assumption predicts <1e-12 while sims show ~1e-4).
- **A. Annechini, A. Barenghi, G. Pelosi, "Estimating the DFR of Binary Regular Codes Using Iterative Decoding", arXiv:2401.16919v4 (29 Jan 2026; IEEE Trans. IT; presented in part at ISIT 2024)**: derives the distribution of syndrome weight before/after the FIRST iteration as function of code parameters and error weight; models bit-flip probabilities at iteration 2; validated in floor and waterfall; improves LEDAcrypt DFR by >2^70.
- **"BIKE_MLThre: A Machine-Learning-Assisted Threshold-Selection Variant of BIKE"** (unrefereed spec-style document, repo ngcc-dev/ngcc-harness, cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/ngcc-dev/ngcc-harness@main/kem-05/kem-05-spec.pdf): learned residual threshold controller ΔT̂_i = f(x_i) with 11 decoder-state features (syndrome weight, iteration index, black/gray set cardinalities, counter-distribution stats), clip to [Tmin,Tmax], confidence gate; offline TEACHER = rollout search ("evaluate a small neighborhood of candidate thresholds around the reference BGF threshold... tested by rollout from the same state") — i.e., counterfactual evaluation BY ROLLOUT; deployed as a fixed small quantized classifier. This is the closest precedent for "learned, feature-based decoder control fitted offline and applied fixed"; it is a control policy, not a failure-risk model, and it needs rollouts, not a surrogate. Provenance/peer-review status unknown.
- Failure-detection in MDPC implementations: **von Maurich, Oder, Güneysu, "Implementing QC-MDPC McEliece Encryption", ACM TECS 14(3), 2015, DOI 10.1145/2700102**: decodable errors decode within ~4-6 iterations; "early detection of decoding failures is possible" (but syndrome-zero check leaks timing -> constant-time practice).
- **J. Chaulet, N. Sendrier, "Worst case QC-MDPC decoder for McEliece cryptosystem", ISIT 2016, arXiv:1608.06080, DOI 10.1109/ISIT.2016.7541522**: syndrome weight is a relevant progress indicator (thresholds as step function of syndrome weight), BUT worst-case behaviour cannot be well predicted from syndrome weight, UPC counts, or number of flips.

Verdict Q1: per-instance failure prediction from (iteration, syndrome-count) features is PUBLISHED (Kim-Park). Early-round statistics as DFR surrogates are PUBLISHED (Paiva; Annechini; Vasseur-Sendrier first-round dominance). NOT found: fitting a per-instance failure-odds model at one operating point and using it COUNTERFACTUALLY to predict risk ratios of other threshold offsets / other operating points. That combination appears novel.

## Q2. Scaling laws / density evolution / phase transitions for parallel BF on MDPC

Waterfall/floor + extrapolation laws:
- **V. Vasseur, PhD thesis "Post-quantum cryptography: a study of the decoding of QC-MDPC codes", Universite de Paris, March 2021** (theses.hal.science/tel-03254461): DFR extrapolation framework: r -> log DFR(r) decreasing, at worst AFFINE outside the error floor; confidence intervals; first-iteration optimum threshold formula T = ceil([d log((1-pi1)/(1-pi0)) + log(|e|/(n-|e|))]/log(...)); weak keys; near-codewords; error floor analysis.
- **V. Vasseur, "QC-MDPC codes DFR and the IND-CCA security of BIKE", ePrint 2021/1458**: extrapolation method from DFRs at two block lengths r, r'; concavity of the waterfall makes 2-point linear extrapolation conservative; two phases of DFR(r): quadratic then linear; validation by holding out an r.
- **N. Drucker, S. Gueron, D. Kostic, "On constant-time QC-MDPC decoding with negligible failure rate", ePrint 2019/1289**: defines BGF + X-DFR extrapolation (simulate small r, fetch r_0 where X-DFR hits 2^-23/2^-64/2^-128); "inspired by the extrapolation method suggested in [18]"; the BIKE team's own methodology.
- **Drucker, Gueron, Kostic, "QC-MDPC decoders with several shades of gray", PQCrypto 2020, LNCS 12100, pp. 35-50, DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-44223-1_3 (ePrint 2019/1423)**: BGF = 1 black-gray + plain BF iterations; 7 steps vs 9 for BG; delta = 4 gray gap; threshold affine in |s| with coefficients depending on w,t not r and not on i.
- **J.-P. Tillich, "The decoding failure probability of MDPC codes", ISIT 2018, arXiv:1801.04668, DOI 10.1109/ISIT.2018.8437843**: majority-logic BF decodes O(sqrt(n) loglog n / log n) errors with zero failure; under Assumption 1, after TWO iterations DFR <= e^{-Omega(n ln ln n / ln n)} when t = Theta(sqrt(n)); unbounded-iteration variant e^{-Omega(n)}. The main published n-scaling law for BF on MDPC.
- **Arpin, Lau, Mesnard, Perlner, Robinson, Tillich, Vasseur, "Error Floor Prediction with Markov Models for QC-MDPC Codes", CRYPTO 2025, ePrint 2025/153, DOI 10.1007/978-3-032-01855-7_8** (slides: iacr.org/submit/files/slides/2025/crypto/crypto2025/556/556_slides.pdf): enriches Sendrier-Vasseur Markov model with near-codeword info; three models incl. "all keys" KEY-DEPENDENT predictions in both regimes; floor dominated by convergence to a near codeword; BIKE DFR ~2^-129.5...2^-136 at r=13477 (vs 12323). Also NIST PQC seminar 2025-01-07 (csrc.nist.gov slides, 365 pp): "single conservative threshold DFR ~2^-90; BIKE 1 +10% r -> 2^-120"; gain from thresholds that decrease during decoding.
- **Arpin, Billingsley, Hast, Lau, Perlner, Robinson, "A Study of Error Floor Behavior in QC-MDPC Codes", PQCrypto 2022, LNCS 13512, pp. 89-103, DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-17234-2_5, ePrint 2022/1043**: experimental floor/waterfall at 20-bit security; failure errors have LOW SYNDROME WEIGHT (differ from random); near-codeword support intersections small (sets insufficient alone). Errata (math.bu.edu/people/drhast) fixes T = max(T', (d+1)/2) implementation bug — useful caution for threshold experiments.
- **Arpin et al., "A graph-theoretic approach to analyzing decoding failures of BIKE", ePrint 2024/1736** (Oct 2024; NIST publication).
- **Baldi, Barenghi, Chiaraluce, Pelosi, Santini, "A Failure Rate Model of Bit-flipping Decoders for QC-LDPC and QC-MDPC Code-based Cryptosystems", SECRYPT 2020, pp. 238-249, DOI 10.5220/0009891702380249**: statistical worst-case model; target 2^-128.
- **Santini, Battaglioni, Baldi, Chiaraluce, "Analysis of the Error Correction Capability of LDPC and MDPC Codes Under Parallel Bit-Flipping Decoding and Application to Cryptography", IEEE Trans. Commun. 68(8):4648-4660, Aug 2020**.
- **Baldi, Barenghi, Chiaraluce, Pelosi, Santini, "Performance Bounds for QC-MDPC Codes Decoders", CBCrypto 2021, LNCS 13150, pp. 95-122, DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-98365-9_6 (ePrint 2021/1557)**; plus "Analysis of In-Place Randomized Bit-Flipping Decoders..." (same group, core.ac.uk 529523981).
- **Annechini, Barenghi, Pelosi, Perriello, ePrint 2025/1043 (rev 2025-12-15) / "Efficient QC-MDPC Cryptosystems with Bounded Decoding Failure Rate", CRYPTO 2026 slides (iacr.org .../2026/crypto/502), Springer DOI 10.1007/978-3-032-35398-6_12**: first closed-form DFR model for the EXACT 3-iteration parallel BF decoder on QC-MDPC; introduces "half codewords" (dominating parallel-decoder floor); per-iteration independence assumptions; example n0=2, v=69, t=130, thresholds (41,35,35) [paper fragment showed 41/37/35 — verify]; states BIKE = 7-iteration BF; explicitly warns that extrapolation from proxy decoders is dangerous; documents that "no closed-form DFR analysis for more than 2 rounds of decoding exists" (before their work).
- **Baldelli, Baldi, Chiaraluce, Santini, "BF-Max: an Efficient Bit Flipping Decoder with Predictable Decoding Failure Rate", IEEE ISIT 2025, arXiv:2506.09689**: flips ONE bit/iteration (max counter); closed-form DFR when IterMax = t (Prop. 4.2); O(r^1.5 log r); inherently constant time; DFR formula tight vs sims; outperforms the 2-iteration bounded decoder of Annechini et al. "Predictable DFR" is prior art for model-based DFR prediction per decoder design.
- Classic density evolution / phase transitions for BF/majority (BSC): **P. Zarrinkhat, A. H. Banihashemi, "Threshold values and convergence properties of majority-based algorithms for decoding regular LDPC codes", IEEE Trans. Commun. 52(12), Dec 2004** (DOI 10.1109/TCOMM.2004.838738): DE for Gallager A/B and majority algorithms; thresholds epsilon*(dv,dc;omega), sometimes smallest root of a polynomial; exponential/super-exponential convergence below threshold.

Verdict Q2: quantitative laws EXIST for (i) DFR vs n (Tillich two-iteration; Vasseur affine-tail extrapolation with confidence intervals), (ii) first-round dominance and first-round syndrome-weight distributions (Vasseur-Sendrier; Annechini et al.; BIKE round-2 spec "Theorem 1" with tau=1/2), (iii) full DFR models for 2- and 3-iteration parallel BF (Annechini et al.), (iv) DE thresholds for classical BF (Zarrinkhat-Banihashemi). NOT found: any published law for the DFR-vs-threshold-offset sensitivity (the "steep two-sided window" in the first round), nor any law that transfers a risk ratio measured at one operating point to another error weight/operating point via per-instance first-round features.

## Q3. BIKE-Flip first-round threshold protection (T_1 = T + delta)

Name/discovery: **BIKE v5.2 = Round-4 submission, version 5.2, 10/10/2024** (BIKE_Spec.2024.10.10.1.pdf, bikesuite.org/files/v5.2/; KAT.2024.10.10.1.zip). v5.2 replaces BGF with the **BIKE-Flip decoder** — "BIKE-Flip Decoder with strengthened resilience to weak keys" listed as [New] in **N. Sendrier, "BIKE Decoding Failure, Weak Keys & Error Floors", NIST PQC seminar, 27 Sep 2024 (HAL hal-04914446)**.

Threshold schedule (spec content; verified through Sendrier's seminar + arXiv:2412.11997 + secondary descriptions; exact v5.2 section number NOT directly verified — see access note):
- T = max( f(w), F_Iter(w_0) ), where w = current syndrome weight, w_0 = initial syndrome weight, Iter = iteration index; IterMax = 7 (Level 1).
- Level 1: f(w) = 0.006254868353074983*w + 11.10143233724 (affine approximation of the optimal threshold — the Vasseur-style formula).
- F_1(w_0) = f(w_0) + 3; F_2(w_0) = (1/3)(2 f(w_0) + (v+1)/2) + 3; F_3(w_0) = (1/3)(f(w_0) + 2 (v+1)/2) + 3; F_Iter(w_0) = (v+1)/2 + 3 for Iter >= 4. (v = column weight; (v+1)/2 = majority floor, = 36 for L1.)
- Sendrier seminar equivalent form: T <- max(T_i + delta, f_t(|s|)), f_t(x) = 0.006258x + 11.094, **delta = 3**, NbIter = 7 at level 1.
- So the FIRST-ROUND PROTECTION TERM is T_1 = f(w_0) + delta with delta = 3 (plus the same +3 margin persists in all iterations through the F_Iter schedule). Reproduced verbatim (with M = (d+1)/2) in **"Efficient Layered New Bit-Flipping QC-MDPC Decoder for BIKE Post-Quantum Cryptography", arXiv:2412.11997 (Dec 2024)**: T' <- f(|s|), M <- (d+1)/2; T <- T'+delta, (2T'+M)/3+delta, (T'+2M)/3+delta, M+delta; return max(f(|s|), T).
- Predecessor (v4.x BGF): first iteration = BitFlipIter with tau_0 = Thresh(s) then two masked regret phases with fixed threshold (d+1)/2 + 1 (black, then gray) — **Paiva & Terada, TCHES 2022, Algorithm 3 and Section 4.1**; also Oslo master thesis (duo.uio.no 10852/87158) and Ostrowski thesis. BIKE v4.1 spec: threshold affine in |s|, "the current rule do[es] not depend on the iteration number i". BIKE v4.2: threshold values in Section 2.3; decoder details in Appendix A.2 (esp. A.2.3).
- BIKE Round-2 spec (BIKE-Spec-2019.06.30.1.pdf): "the DFR is dominated by the probability that the first round of the algorithm is unable to reduce significantly the number of initial errors"; its Theorem-1-style bound assumes first-round syndrome-bit independence and a FIXED threshold tau = 1/2 (Backflip era; threshold(S,t') = smallest T satisfying t' binom(d,T) pi1^T (1-pi1)^{d-T} >= (n-t') binom(d,T) pi0^T (1-pi0)^{d-T}, with pi1 = (S+X)/(t'd), pi0 = (wS-X)/((n-t')d)).

Stated justification of the +delta protection (per Sendrier seminar as reported; and Barekeng 2026 secondary analysis):
- Failure mechanism: at iterations 2 (sometimes 3), a few CORRECT positions show extremely high counters ("bogus positions") while the syndrome weight — and hence the threshold — is much lower than expected for the remaining error weight; those bogus positions get flipped and irreparably spoil decoding.
- The delta/iteration-dependent floor keeps the threshold raised in early iterations (low confidence) to prevent premature flips; purpose = fewer wrong flips, failure patterns less predictable/exploitable => "strengthened resilience to weak keys" (motivated by the predictable failure patterns exploited by Wang-Wang-Wang CRYPTO 2023 m-gathering weak keys).
- Secondary source phrasing (Barekeng 2026, "MATHEMATICAL ANALYSIS OF QC-MDPC STRUCTURES IN BIKE V5.2...", J. Math. Appl. 20(2):1061-1076, DOI 10.30598/barekengvol20iss2pp1061-1076): "In the first iteration, the threshold is defined as T_1(S_0) = f_t(S_0) + delta... A higher threshold in the first iteration allows for the correction of more errors... reducing the possibility of exploitation by an attacker." (Their wording is internally inconsistent about flipping more/fewer; treat the spec formula as authoritative.)

Published QUANTITATIVE analysis of it:
- **Sendrier NIST seminar (hal-04914446)**: with delta = 3, NbIter = 7, waterfall DFR extrapolation at Level 1 ~ 2^-181.
- **Vasseur (thesis 2021; ePrint 2021/1458; Sendrier-Vasseur PQCrypto 2019)**: the theory behind f(w) — optimal first-round threshold balancing P(flip wrong) vs P(flip right) given syndrome weight and residual error weight; counter distributions conditioned on syndrome weight; first-round optimality (2017 thesis excerpt: first-round threshold model "seems reliable"; second iteration is where fidelity breaks).
- **Arpin et al. CRYPTO 2025**: "bad bits and suspicious bits" / near-codeword convergence models that explain the early-iteration failure modes the delta protects against (the NCW-aware paper explicitly recalls "bad bits and suspicious bits from [12]" = Arpin et al.).
- NOT found: any peer-reviewed paper measuring the DFR/risk sensitivity to the first-round offset itself (e.g., delta+1, delta+2) — i.e., our "FRO-2/+2 experiment" target quantity has no published quantitative analysis. Note also that **FRO-2 (+2 in the first iteration ONLY) is NOT the spec's scheme**: the spec's protection is +3 applied through the iteration schedule (F_1..F_{>=4} all carry +3); the older BGF used the fixed (d+1)/2+1 masked-iteration floor. Be precise about this in the paper.

## Q4. Rare-event DFR estimation (importance sampling) and surrogate precedents

- **C. A. Cole, S. G. Wilson, E. K. Hall, T. R. Giallorenzi, "A General Method for Finding Low Error Rates of LDPC Codes", arXiv:cs/0605051 (2006; IEEE Trans. Comm. 2009)**: three-step IS (trapping-set local search; error-boundary measurement; mean-translation IS); needs known dominant TS and n <~ 10^4.
- **T. Richardson, "Error Floors of LDPC Codes", Allerton 2003**: semi-analytical IS — the first error-floor estimation method.
- **D. Declercq, M. P. C. Fossorier, "Improved impulse method to evaluate the low weight profile of sparse binary linear codes", IEEE ISIT 2008, pp. 1963-1967, DOI 10.1109/ISIT.2008.4595332**: impulse method + conditioned MRB + OSD re-encoding for low-weight profile/error floor.
- **B. Vasic, D. Declercq, S. Planjery et al., "LDPC Error Floor Prediction using Trapping Set aware Code Shortening", NVMW 2019** (nvmw.ucsd.edu): simulation on a shortened code retaining dominant TSs; ~1e4 speedup; decoder-agnostic.
- "Controlling the Error Floor in LDPC Decoding", arXiv:1209.0532: over-biasing caveats for mean-shift IS.
- DAIS / Fast-Flat-Histogram adaptive-IS methods for low FER of LDPC (general rare-event toolkit).
- **No published application of importance sampling to QC-MDPC/cryptographic DFR found.** Instead, the field uses: (i) high-DFR simulation + extrapolation (DKG 2019/1289; Vasseur 2021/1458; BIKE spec); (ii) closed-form models (Annechini; BF-Max); (iii) Markov + near-codeword models (Arpin et al.); and states explicitly that the target "(does) not allow Monte Carlo simulations" (CRYPTO 2026 slides).
- Surrogate-statistic precedents for predicting DFR at unmeasurable levels: Paiva & Terada's average-errors-left-after-first-iteration (TCHES 2022) and Annechini et al.'s syndrome-weight distribution before/after the first iteration (arXiv:2401.16919). Our per-instance odds surrogate is a finer-grained version of these.

## Q5. Key-conditional DFR without decoding

- Weak-key framework: **N. Sendrier, V. Vasseur, "On the Existence of Weak Keys for QC-MDPC Decoding", ePrint 2020/1232 (IACR Cryptol. ePrint Arch. 2020; HAL hal-03139708)**: generalizes Type-I weak keys: h_i = phi_delta(x^l (1+x+...+x^{f-1}) + h'_i); abnormal distance-spectrum multiplicities (mu(delta) >= f-1, mu(2delta) >= f-2, ...); mechanism: errors hide each other, correct positions contaminate neighbours; bound |W_I(f)| <= 2r floor(r/2) binom(r-f, d-f); weak-set contribution below 2^-lambda requirement (weak set W with DFR delta and density tau is harmless if delta*tau < 2^-lambda).
- Type I/II/III classification: "A combinatorial approach to avoiding weak keys in the BIKE cryptosystem", arXiv:2410.11111 (2024; par.nsf.gov 10618533): Type I = many consecutive nonzeros (origin: Drucker-Gueron-Kostic 2019); Type II = periodic structure (one distance multiplicity >= m; Vasseur 2021); Type III = inter-block distance multiplicity; relates weak keys to 4-cycles in the Tanner graph; proposes a key filter.
- Attacks: **Q. Guo, T. Johansson, P. Stankovski, ASIACRYPT 2016** (key-recovery via decoding errors, LNCS 10031, pp. 789-815); **T. Wang, A. Wang, X. Wang, "Exploring Decryption Failures of BIKE: New Class of Weak Keys and Key Recovery Attacks", CRYPTO 2023, LNCS 14083, pp. 70-100**: m-gathering weak keys, DFR_weak >> DFR_avg (e.g. (m,eps)=(4000,1): density 2^-87.28, DFR 2^-29.33), multi-target recovery with 2^117 oracle queries; also establishes BIKE L1 average DFR >= ~2^-116.61 (2^11.39 above requirement).
- **A. Mesnard, N. Sendrier, "Security of BIKE against failure attacks"** (members.loria.fr/EThome/c2/595696.pdf; JC2 2025 abstract): spectrum of m-gathering keys highly unusual; strong correlation between DFR and spectrum properties; explores near-codewords vs decoding failures vs weak-key spectra (refers to Arpin et al. models as work in progress).
- **T. Gellersen, T. Eifert, S. Berndt, T. Eisenbarth, "Efficient Weak Key Recovery for QC-MDPC Codes like BIKE", IACR Communications in Cryptology 2(3), 2025, DOI 10.62056/a0qj5wol7**: Type-I weak keys feature a distinct distance spectrum; new reaction-attack recovery; 1e6 traces (128-bit) vs 4e7 for random keys; BIKE-Flip does not remove the Type-I weakness ("only a minimal impact on their DFR"); BIKE-Flip "omits the Black-Grey preprocessing step and employs a different threshold schedule... motivated by the belief that the BIKE-Flip decoder offers improved resistance against weak keys (Wang et al. CRYPTO 2023)".
- Key-dependent DFR prediction: **Arpin et al. CRYPTO 2025** — "refined model for all keys" giving key-dependent predictions in both waterfall and floor regimes (model-based, no decoding at the target point).
- Near-codeword-aware decoding (structural, not predictive): **A. Baldelli, M. Baldi, D. De Zuane, P. Santini, "Near-Codewords Aware Bit Flipping Decoding of QC-MDPC Codes", arXiv:2604.18247 (Apr 2026)**: near-codeword syndromes known in advance -> recognize bad configuration from syndrome -> lookup-table correction; almost-near-codewords are the dominant failure events at low DFR; modified BF-Max never failed in simulations. Also **Santini, De Zuane, Baldelli, Baldi, "Flip a Failure into a Success: Improved Bit Flipping Decoding for QC-MDPC Codes", ePrint 2026/1616** (Aug 2026).
- Classic trapping-set apparatus: Richardson, "Error Floors of LDPC Codes" (Allerton 2003); Dolecek, Zhang, Anantharam, Wainwright, Nikolic, "Analysis of absorbing sets and fully absorbing sets of array-based LDPC codes", IEEE Trans. IT 56(1), 2010.
- **Barbero**: NO QC-MDPC DFR/near-codeword work found under that name in any search. (Closest actual names: Baldi/Baldelli/Barenghi/Santini; if the intended name was "Barbero", the lead does not check out.)

## VERDICT on the surrogate-model idea

1. INGREDIENTS all exist: per-instance failure prediction from syndrome-state features (Kim-Park CCNC 2021, LDPC); first-round-dominated DFR theory (Sendrier-Vasseur 2019; Vasseur 2021); first-round surrogate statistics (Paiva TCHES 2022; Annechini 2401.16919); learned decoder-state-driven threshold control (BIKE_MLThre doc, with offline rollout-based "counterfactual" teacher search); key-conditional DFR models (Arpin et al. CRYPTO 2025); near-codeword failure characterization (Vasseur; Arpin; Baldelli et al. 2026).
2. The specific COMBINATION — fit a per-instance failure-odds model P(fail | round-1 flips, post-round-1 syndrome weight) at ONE operating point, compute features WITHOUT running the counterfactual arm, and use the fitted model to PREDICT RISK RATIOS for other first-round offsets and other operating points (incl. unmeasurable DFR at BIKE L1) — was NOT found in published literature. This is the defensible novelty; pitch it as a "counterfactual surrogate" / "risk-transfer" methodology, not as new failure prediction, not as a new first-round-protection discovery.
3. BIGGEST RISK to the novelty claim: the BIKE-Flip spec already contains a first-round threshold protection (F_1(w_0) = f(w_0) + delta, delta = 3; equivalently T <- max(T_i + delta, f_t(|s|)) with the "bogus positions" justification) and the failure mechanism it addresses is published (Sendrier seminar 2024; Wang et al. 2023). If the paper's FRO-2 is presented as discovering first-round threshold protection, it will be read as re-deriving the v5.2 spec's delta; the novelty must live in the counterfactual per-instance surrogate + the two-sided steep window + sustained-offset destruction measurements and the unmeasurable-DFR risk-ratio transfer. Secondary risks: (a) Kim-Park already fits per-instance failure odds on syndrome features; (b) Paiva already uses a first-round surrogate statistic for DFR comparison; (c) BIKE_MLThre already demonstrates learned decoder-state -> threshold control with offline counterfactual teacher search (even if unrefereed); (d) Arpin et al. already give key-dependent DFR predictions without decoding at the target point.
